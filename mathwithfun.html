Visualizing Mathematics

Exploring geometry, sequences, waves, topology, chaos theory, and 3D projection through dynamic visual code.

The Fibonacci Spiral

By drawing quarter-circles inside squares sized by the Fibonacci sequence (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13...), we approximate the Golden Spiral. This logarithmic spiral manifests everywhere in nature, from sunflower seed patterns to the shapes of galaxies.

Sequence: 1, 1

Fourier Series: Square Wave

Fourier theory dictates that any periodic wave can be broken down into an infinite sum of simple sine waves. Watch how adding rotating epicycles (circles riding on circles) slowly deforms a smooth curve into a sharp square wave.

Active Orbits: 1 (Fundamental Frequency)

The Möbius Strip (3D)

A classic object in topology, a Möbius strip is a surface with only one side and one boundary component. If you trace a line down the middle, you will travel along both "sides" and end up right back where you started without ever crossing over an edge.

Equation Parameter: θ variant [0 to 2π], w variant [-1 to 1]

The Lorenz Attractor

Derived from atmospheric equations by Edward Lorenz, this system represents deterministic chaos. Even though the rules are completely fixed, slight variations in starting points trigger drastically wild pathways, tracing out a pattern resembling butterfly wings.

dx/dt = σ(y - x), dy/dt = x(ρ - z) - y

The Ulam Spiral

Discovered by Stanislaw Ulam during a boring meeting, this is a spiral grid layout of integers. When you color only the prime numbers, they unexpectedly form crisp, non-random diagonal lines. It hints at hidden mathematical structures deep within the distribution of primes.

Chaos Game: Fractals

Start with 3 vertices of a triangle. Pick a random point inside. Repeatedly roll a 3-sided die, move halfway toward that vertex, and draw a dot. Out of pure randomness, an infinitely complex fractal structure appears!

3D Graphics Engine: Projection

How do 3D video games exist on flat screens? This engine takes raw 3D coordinate vectors (X, Y, Z), multiplies them by Rotation Matrices using sine and cosine, and applies perspective division to map them to 2D screen pixels. Click and drag the canvas to manually rotate space!
